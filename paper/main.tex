\begin{abstract}
One fixed finite-range, translation-invariant, time-periodic quantum circuit on a one-dimensional ring samples the computational-basis output of any width-$n$, depth-$D$ Toffoli/Hadamard circuit on the all-zero input within total variation error $\varepsilon$. The ring size and number of periods are at most $A(n+D+\log_2(1/\varepsilon))^A$ for a fixed $A$, and the per-site register is fixed. The polynomial-time computable product preparation puts the program in classical basis registers and initializes all quantum registers to zero; a deterministic polynomial-time classical decoder uses only $n,D$ and a sufficient integer precision request. Independent wire depolarization or whole-site replacement acts after every elementary layer, including on classical control, below a fixed positive threshold. Theorem D extends the accuracy and polynomial resource bounds to sufficiently small general local fault-path noise, including coherent errors and correlated bath interactions satisfying a fault-path superset bound. Its threshold is positive but astronomically small. The proof combines exact recovery on certified supports, multiscale dense-witness counting for overlapping long tests, and the established fault-path superset method. Noiseless recursive encoding and recovery give the corresponding reference-entangled input/output guarantee. An elementary register-bank corollary replaces the periodic word by one fixed channel at every step, preserves noise after every original layer, and stores a reference-entangled qubit with fidelity at least $15/16$ through a horizon linear in ring size. Its ideal onsite bank shift is a noiseless timing structure; noise within a decomposition of that shift and times beyond the horizon are not covered. The construction follows the one-dimensional route proposed by D\"unnweber, Styliaris and Trivedi for their two-dimensional construction, which already treats general local fault-path noise with polylogarithmic overhead. Our overhead is polynomial, with very large constants and an initialized synchronous hierarchy.
\end{abstract}
