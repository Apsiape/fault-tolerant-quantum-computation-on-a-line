\section{Scope, limitations and counting restrictions}\label{sec:discussion}
The contribution is a fixed translation-invariant, time-periodic noisy rule on a line with a product preparation and a gate-independent classical output decoder. The initialized hierarchy and synchronous schedule remain part of the model. The polynomial bound is an existence bound; code sizes, local registers, ranges, periods and the inverse threshold can be extremely large. Near-linear space and time of the form $(n+D)\operatorname{polylog}((n+D)/\varepsilon)$ remain open here. The finite chart pays for the whole physical time and proves no storage lifetime beyond linear in ring length. These are distinct open improvements; neither follows by iterating a one-tick storage estimate.

Classical program placement in a quantum automaton has noiseless precedents in Arrighi, Fargetton and Wang~\cite{AFW2009}, Shepherd, Franz and Werner~\cite{SFW2006}, and Vollbrecht and Cirac~\cite{VC2006,VC2008}. Nagaj and Wocjan~\cite{NW2008} give computation by a fixed one-dimensional Hamiltonian from programmed product input, and Kohler, Piddock, Bausch and Cubitt~\cite{KPBC2022} establish related translation-invariant Hamiltonian universality. These works do not provide the continuing-noise theorem proved here. Fitzsimons and Twamley~\cite{FT2007} and Kay~\cite{Kay2007} establish one-dimensional global-control thresholds with an externally scheduled pulse sequence, and Kay~\cite{Kay2005} treats errors in the control unit of such schemes. Kay~\cite{Kay2007} and P\'erez Delgado~\cite{PerezDelgado2007} also discuss fault-tolerant quantum cellular automata as an open direction. Harrington's hierarchical local decoding~\cite{Harrington2004} is an early quantum-memory precedent, with reliable classical processing rather than the all-noisy control of the present model.

The two-dimensional construction of D\"unnweber, Styliaris and Trivedi~\cite{DST2026} already has the principal fixed-rule computation features. The dimension reduction here follows their proposed one-dimensional route. We retain a prescribed hierarchical classical boundary, so no self-organization result is obtained. The open directions of Balasubramanian, Davydova and Lake~\cite{BDL2026} include constant rate and continuous-time dynamics as well as translation invariance and universal computation; this paper addresses the latter features in the one-dimensional synchronous periodic form. It proves neither constant rate nor a time-independent continuous-time implementation. This distinction also follows the taxonomy of Drechsler, Oreg and Aharonov~\cite{DOA2026}. The stronger efficiency target raised by Gidney and Bergamaschi~\cite{GB2025} remains open in this model.

The elementary gate set deliberately contains non-Clifford Toffoli and explicit reset. The rapid forgetting theorem for noisy Clifford circuits of Nelson, Rajakumar and Gullans~\cite{NRG2026} therefore does not cover this rule. Resets are nonunital operations, as needed to escape the limitations of Aharonov, Ben-Or, Impagliazzo and Nisan~\cite{ABIN1996} for noisy reversible circuits whose inputs are all present at time zero. The thermal mixing results of Bergamaschi and Chen~\cite{BC2025}, Chen and Ding~\cite{CD2026}, and Bardet and collaborators~\cite{Bardet2023} concern detailed-balance dynamics; the present driven circuit has no such hypothesis. The local stabilizer Hamiltonian and code restrictions of Bravyi and Terhal~\cite{BravyiTerhal2009} and Bravyi, Poulin and Terhal~\cite{BPT2010} likewise do not give a restriction on this active circuit with nonlocal encoded stabilizers. These model distinctions do not assert a threshold for noise beyond \eqref{eq:wire-noise} and \eqref{eq:site-noise}.

\begin{proposition}[Measurement-alphabet bound]\label{prop:counting}
If a deterministic decoder of $M_{\rm out}$ measured bits approximates $n$ independent unbiased bits within total variation distance $\varepsilon<1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counting}
 M_{\rm out}\ge n+\log_2(1-\varepsilon).
\end{equation}
In particular $Q_{\rm site}L$ must satisfy this bound for the depth-one circuit of $n$ Hadamards.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The decoder's image contains at most $2^{M_{\rm out}}$ outputs. The uniform law on $\{0,1\}^n$ assigns that image mass at most $2^{M_{\rm out}-n}$, whereas the decoded law assigns it mass one. Their total variation distance is at least $1-2^{M_{\rm out}-n}$. Rearranging gives \eqref{eq:counting}. The initial noise or probabilities of the measured strings cannot enlarge the deterministic image. The additive resource argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is always at least $n$ at positive width, even as $\varepsilon$ tends to one, and is compatible with this restriction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite precision descriptions and empty width]\label{prop:precision}
The numerical guarantee of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} cannot be combined with losslessly recoverable descriptions of every rational tolerance in the same bounded classical basis preparation. If width and depth both zero are admitted with a nonempty ring, replace the resource argument by $\max(1,n+D)+\log_2(1/\varepsilon)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $n=D=1$ and a target circuit. For all $\varepsilon\in(1/2,1)$ the bound is at most $A3^A$ sites. A finite classical basis alphabet on any of those finitely many ring lengths gives only finitely many preparations, even if length itself is side information. Infinitely many distinct dyadic rational tolerances already contradict lossless recovery. Storing a sufficient integer request instead gives the full numerical guarantee for every tolerance, as Section~\ref{sec:precision} specifies; it does not encode that real number exactly.

If $n=D=0$, for any fixed $A$ the unfloored bound $A(\log_2(1/\varepsilon))^A$ becomes smaller than one as $\varepsilon$ tends to one, although a nonempty ring has at least one site. With the floored argument use one site and the empty-output decoder, whose error is zero. If a physical request field is required, $h+1$ sites record it in unary; since $h+1\le X+2\le3X$ for the floored $X=1+\log_2(1/\varepsilon)$, the constant $A$ of \eqref{eq:A} already covers this case. The positive-width theorem and its displayed formula are unchanged. Depth zero at positive width is already included by $B=(7n+8)(nD+1)$.
\end{proof}

The counting and representability restrictions are basic obstructions, not additional efficiency claims. The established extensions are preservation of arbitrary reference-entangled inputs with noiseless endpoints and the common-threshold whole-site model. Stronger overhead, lifetime and initialization results require further arguments.
