\begin{abstract}
One fixed, finite-range, translation-invariant, time-periodic quantum circuit on a ring performs any width-$n$, depth-$D$ Toffoli/Hadamard circuit from a computable product preparation, with space and time at most $A(n+D+\log_2(1/\varepsilon))^A$ and output error at most $\varepsilon$. The register per site and $A$ are fixed. Independent depolarizing noise acts on every wire after every elementary layer, including every classical control wire, at any rate below a fixed positive threshold. The target circuit enters only through classical basis registers; all quantum registers start in zero. A deterministic classical decoder, depending only on $n,D$ and a sufficient integer precision request, reads the final computational-basis measurement. A serial quantum processor is embedded in an initialized one-dimensional classical self-simulating hierarchy. Coordinatewise private teleportation corrections load repeated classical words without assuming that they are quantum codewords, and a closed tape-end rule confines faulty instruction decisions to one contact. With noiseless recursive encoding and recovery at the endpoints, the same circuit preserves arbitrary input entanglement with a reference. Independent whole-site replacement has a common positive threshold on the same hardware. This is a one-dimensional analogue of the two-dimensional construction of D\"unnweber, Styliaris and Trivedi, following their suggested route through G\'acs's one-dimensional hierarchy. Our overhead is polynomial rather than polylogarithmic. The hierarchy is initialized and synchronous; the constants are extremely large.
\end{abstract}
